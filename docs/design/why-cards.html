Why was this slide chosen?

Four ways to show the evidence behind a result card, drawn with real data from one search. Each design renders the same five slides: the top result, the second, the fourth, the ninth, and the Instagram video slide that used to be first (now 40th, carried only by a keyword hit). Pick one, or a combination.

Distance scale across this index: on-topic answers land at about 0.35 to 0.50; above 0.55 the page calls the results weak. The score is 1/(60 + meaning rank) + ½ · 1/(60 + keyword rank).

A Evidence ledger

Every item of the slide that matched, one row each: what kind, how close (bar), and which rank it took on each side. The best sentence from the closest item is quoted. Items that exist on the slide but did not match are named, so an absence is visible too.

Good for: seeing at a glance whether a slide won on one strong item or several weak ones, and whether the narration or the picture carried it. Cost: the card gets taller when a slide has many narration chunks.

B Heat text

The full text of the closest item, with each sentence shaded by how close it is to the question on its own, and the question's words underlined where they appear. This answers "which text matched" directly.

Good for: troubleshooting a surprising hit: the shading shows the one sentence the embedding latched on to. Cost: one extra embedding call per page of results (all sentences in one batch, about 0.2 s); reads as a wall of text when the item is long.

C Distance gauge

One ruler per card. Every matched item is a dot on the distance scale, colored by kind; the big dot is the one that ranked the slide. The red line is the weak-match threshold and the shaded zone beyond it is where results stop being trusted. The best sentence sits under it.

Good for: comparing cards against each other and against the threshold with one glance; compact. Cost: keyword-only hits have no distance, so they show as a note rather than a dot.

D In words, with the score split

A plain sentence that states the reason ("ranked first because its narration was the closest item in the whole index; no keyword match"), followed by the score drawn as a bar split into its meaning part and its keyword part, and the best sentence.

Good for: anyone who does not want to read numbers; the sentence names the exact cause. Cost: the split bar is only interesting when both sides contributed, which is rare for long questions.

Reply with a letter, or a combination. Suggested pairings: D + C (the sentence says why, the ruler shows how strong) or A + B (the ledger lists the evidence, the heat text shows where in it). Any of these can be the default view or sit behind a "why?" toggle on the card.
